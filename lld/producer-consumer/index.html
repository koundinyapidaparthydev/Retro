Producer–consumer

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Producer–consumer.” They describe this:

Given: Each email does new Thread. The ingest side is bursty; SMTP is slow. The list between them has no cap.

Find: A bounded queue, a full-queue policy, and consumers that keep pulling after a failed item.

Tiny example: Queue of 100, reject or block. Immutable messages. Poison pill or close() to drain.

Name of the tool

Producers put work on a bounded queue; consumers take it. Decouple rates and isolate failures with a clear backpressure story.

Why this tool

This is the default concurrency design in LLD: log appenders, job workers, notification senders, crawlers.

Approach

  1. Define the work item (immutable message).
  2. Choose a bounded BlockingQueue (or implement wait/notify on a ring).
  3. Producers: offer/put with a policy — block, timeout, drop, reject.

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • Name the bound and what happens when it is full.
  • A pool of consumers, not a thread per park().
  • Logger that enqueues; one writer thread.

You say this first

I wouldn't name the pattern first. I'd say the problem producer–consumer solves, then the three types. Producers put work on a bounded queue; consumers take it. Decouple rates and isolate failures with a clear backpressure story.

Shape: Define the work item (immutable message).. Don't: Synchronous request/response where the caller must have the result in-line — use a Future, or just call. Always name the bound and the full-queue policy. That is the senior sentence.

More notes if you want them

This is the default concurrency design in LLD: log appenders, job workers, notification senders, crawlers. If you cannot explain the queue bound and what happens when it is full, you do not have a design — you have a thread leak.

Use when: Any time one side is bursty: ingest vs write, UI vs IO, requests vs emails.

Skip when: Synchronous request/response where the caller must have the result in-line — use a Future, or just call.

Trap: Unbounded queues.
