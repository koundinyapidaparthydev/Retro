The problem they give you

They will not say “Pattern lookalikes.” They describe this:

Given: They ask 'is that not just a decorator?' You drew a wrapper. Strategy and State both have a field. Factory Method and Abstract Factory are both named Factory.

Find: Distinguish on intent, not on whether there is a wrapper field. Say the sentence, then keep coding.

Tiny example: Retry stack is extra behavior; ACL stand-in is access. Stripe→port is shape; park() is a simpler API over many types. One product vs a matching family. Fan-out vs a named hub.

Name of the tool

Pairs people confuse on the whiteboard: Decorator vs Proxy, Strategy vs State, Adapter vs Facade, Factory Method vs Abstract Factory, Observer vs Mediator vs Event bus.

Why this tool

Interviewers often ask 'is that not just X?'.

Approach

  1. When you name a pattern, add a half-sentence of intent.
  2. If they say 'is that X?', compare on intent, not on whether there is a wrapper field.
  3. Prefer the smaller pattern when both apply (Strategy over Bridge for one axis).

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • Is that not just X? Compare intent, not the wrapper field.
  • Fee types as State — wrong. How I price vs what I am.
  • Undo: inverse command vs opaque snapshot. Which and why?

You say this first

I wouldn't name the pattern first. I'd say the problem pattern lookalikes solves, then the three types. Pairs people confuse on the whiteboard: Decorator vs Proxy, Strategy vs State, Adapter vs Facade, Factory Method vs Abstract Factory, Observer vs Mediator vs Event bus.

Shape: When you name a pattern, add a half-sentence of intent.. Don't: Spending five minutes distinguishing diamonds in UML when the use case is uncoded. Memorize these five pairs. They cover most 'gotcha' questions.

Interviewers often ask 'is that not just X?'. Distinguishing lookalikes is how you show you understand intent, not class diagrams you memorized.

Use when: Any time you are about to draw two wrappers.

Skip when: Spending five minutes distinguishing diamonds in UML when the use case is uncoded.

Trap: Treating all wrappers as Adapter.
