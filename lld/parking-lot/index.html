Parking lot

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Parking lot.” They describe this:

Given: A mall garage takes cars and bikes on several floors. Drivers get a ticket and pay on the way out. Spots have types.

Find: Lot owns floors and spots. park/unpark atomically. Fees are a policy, not a switch on the spot.

Tiny example: Weekend rates and EV stalls. Last stall, two cars — one ticket. Do not rewrite park.

Name of the tool

A lot owns floors and spots. A car asks for a matching free spot, gets a ticket, and pays a fee policy when it leaves.

Why this tool

This is the canonical LLD.

Approach

  1. Confirm types of spots/vehicles, payment, multi-floor, reservations.
  2. Draw Lot → Floor → Spot; Ticket; FeePolicy interface.
  3. Implement find+occupy atomically on the lot.

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • Design a parking garage. Classes, then I add a vehicle type.
  • EV spots and surge pricing without editing Lot.park.
  • Make park safe when two cars want the last stall.

You say this first

v1 verbs: park and unpark. The lot owns floors and spots. A ticket records the stay. Fees are a FeePolicy, not a switch in unpark. occupy() enforces one vehicle per spot.

When they add EV, add a spot type and a finder preference. Weekend rates = new policy. Last-spot race: lock the lot or the floor around find+occupy.

More notes if you want them

This is the canonical LLD. It tests modeling, invariants (occupied iff vehicle), OCP (new vehicle or fee), and a clean service API. If you can extend it live, you can do most object designs.

Use when: Warm-up LLD, teaching aggregates, any 'resource assignment + billing' problem.

Skip when: Do not turn it into a city-scale HLD unless they ask — stay in-process.

Trap: Public spot list mutated by the caller.
