Euclid GCD and LCM

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Euclid GCD and LCM.” They describe this:

Given: Two integers a and b.

Find: Their greatest common divisor and LCM. Optionally integers x, y with ax + by = gcd.

Tiny example: gcd(48, 18) = 6, lcm = 144. Jugs of 3 and 5 can measure 4 because gcd = 1.

Name of the tool

gcd(a,b) = gcd(b, a mod b), then 0. lcm(a,b) = a / gcd * b to avoid overflow. The extended algorithm also finds Bézout coefficients for modular inverses.

Approach

  1. while b: [a,b] = [b, a%b]; return a.
  2. lcm = a / gcd(a,b) * b (abs, and zero-safe).
  3. Extended: recurse, then x = y1, y = x1 - q*y1 where q = floor(a/b).

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • GCD and LCM of two numbers.
  • Can these two jugs measure exactly z liters?
  • Find x and y so that ax + by equals the gcd.

You say this first

gcd(a,b) = gcd(b, a mod b), then 0. lcm(a,b) = a / gcd * b to avoid overflow. The extended algorithm also finds Bézout coefficients for modular inverses. I'd dry-run a tiny case first, then say the invariant and the complexity (O(log min(a,b)) time, O(1) iterative; O(log) recursive extended space).

Offer the next notch: lcm = a / gcd(a,b) * b (abs, and zero-safe).. Tradeoff: You need all prime factors — factorize, do not only gcd. Water-and-jugs: measurable iff target is a multiple of gcd(x,y) (and ≤ x+y).

More notes if you want them

GCD is the number-theory primitive interviews actually use: reduce fractions, lcm of run lengths, cycle alignment, and 'greatest common divisor traversal' graph problems. If you write a factor loop to min(a,b) you will TLE. Euclid is logarithmic. Extended Euclid is how you invert a modulo m when gcd=1 — the other half of modular arithmetic interviews.

Use when: Reduce ratios, align cycles, modular inverse when m is not prime, lattice steps.

Skip when: You need all prime factors — factorize, do not only gcd.

Trap: lcm as a*b/gcd overflowing before the divide.
