The problem they give you

They will not say “Sagas: choreography vs orchestration.” They describe this:

Given: Booking a trip writes to flights, hotels, and payments. The hotel call fails after the flight is held.

Find: Design a multi-service workflow that can compensate, not a cross-DB transaction.

Tiny example: Three services, 800 bookings/s, payment captured, hotel 500s.

Name of the tool

Long-running workflows across services without 2PC — a sequence of local transactions plus compensations when a step fails.

Why this tool

Checkout, booking, and onboarding span payments, inventory, and email.

Approach

  1. Write the happy path and a compensation for each mutating step (release seat, refund, unreserve SKU).
  2. Pick choreography for 2–3 obvious events; orchestrate when there are timeouts, branches, or humans.
  3. Persist saga state (id, step, version); never only in memory.

You say this first

Long-running workflows across services without 2PC — a sequence of local transactions plus compensations when a step fails. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Checkout, trip booking, KYC, multi-step money movement.

Tradeoff: Choreography: fewer moving parts, poorer observability and control.. Failure mode: No timeout on a reservation — leaked inventory. For Ticketmaster/Uber/payments, draw the saga states on the board.

More notes if you want them

Checkout, booking, and onboarding span payments, inventory, and email. Distributed 2PC is rarely the HLD answer. Sagas are — if you name compensations and who drives the state machine.

Use when: Checkout, trip booking, KYC, multi-step money movement.

Skip when: A single-row update in one DB — just use a transaction.

Trap: No timeout on a reservation — leaked inventory.
