Webhooks vs polling

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Webhooks vs polling.” They describe this:

Given: Partners need to know when a payout completes. They will either hammer GET or give you a URL to POST.

Find: Pick push vs pull, and design retries, signatures, and a portal to replay.

Tiny example: 2k partners, 30% endpoints are down at any time, a webhook they must not forge.

Name of the tool

Push callbacks when something happens versus the client asking 'any news?' — including signatures, retries, and when to offer both.

Why this tool

Partner integrations and 'is my job done?' UX live here.

Approach

  1. Define an event catalog and a stable payload version.
  2. If webhook: sign, retry, DLQ, timeout (~3–10s), and a replay API.
  3. If poll: cursor/pagination, rate limits, and a recommended interval.

You say this first

Push callbacks when something happens versus the client asking 'any news?' — including signatures, retries, and when to offer both. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Webhooks: Stripe-like platforms, CI callbacks, 'payment settled.'

Tradeoff: Poll: simple and robust, waste and lag.. Failure mode: Retries that storm a customer who is down (no backoff, no circuit). For notifications and public API, mention signed webhooks + replay + poll backup.

More notes if you want them

Partner integrations and 'is my job done?' UX live here. Polling is simple and wasteful. Webhooks are efficient and operationally spicy (downtime, replay, SSRF). Public API platforms are judged on this design.

Use when: Webhooks: Stripe-like platforms, CI callbacks, 'payment settled.'

Skip when: Do not poll every 200ms from 2M mobile clients for a rare event — use push/WS.

Trap: Retries that storm a customer who is down (no backoff, no circuit).
