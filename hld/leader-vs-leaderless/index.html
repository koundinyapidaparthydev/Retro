Leader vs leaderless replication

NEXT

Definition

A single primary sequences writes, or every replica can take writes. The choice is latency, conflict handling, and how you fail over.

Why it exists

Almost every datastore is one of these two shapes.

Approach

  1. If invariants need a single order, pick a leader (global or per shard/key).
  2. If the workload is multi-region writes of independent keys, consider leaderless or per-key leaders.
  3. Plan failover: lease + fencing for leaderful; repair + read merge for leaderless.

More notes if you want them

Almost every datastore is one of these two shapes. Leaderful is easier to reason about and easier to hotspot. Leaderless is how you stay available across DCs — if you can merge. Saying 'we replicate' without this split is incomplete.

Use when: Leaderful: RDBMS, Raft logs, Kafka partitions, any serializable local transaction.

Skip when: Do not run multi-leader on a wallet without a deterministic merge (there isn't one for money).

Trap: Two primaries after a netsplit (split-brain) without quorum or STONITH.
