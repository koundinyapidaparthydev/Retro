The problem they give you

They will not say “Retention and deletion.” They describe this:

Given: Events have piled up for five years. Legal wants some gone; product wants “we never lose history.”

Find: Pick retention per store and a delete path that actually reaches replicas and backups.

Tiny example: Hot clicks 30 days, raw logs 13 months, warehouse 5 years, a GDPR delete tomorrow.

Name of the tool

How long each class of data lives, how it is deleted everywhere, and how you avoid infinite disks and immortal PII.

Why this tool

Every log, object, tombstone, and warehouse table needs a lifetime.

Approach

  1. Label TTL/retention on every store in the design.
  2. Automate expire (DB partitions, S3 lifecycle, Kafka retention).
  3. Implement user-delete as a saga with retries and a completion record.

Tradeoff: Short retention: cheaper and more private, worse forensics and ML.. Failure mode: S3 versioning on with no lifecycle — delete is a lie. Add a column 'TTL' on your tables in the interview — it is memorable.

More notes if you want them

Every log, object, tombstone, and warehouse table needs a lifetime. Retention is cost, privacy, and sometimes law (keep invoices 7 years, delete GPS after 30 days). HLD tables should show TTL next to PK.

Use when: All systems — this is default design, not a GDPR-only add-on.

Skip when: Do not TTL the ledger.

Trap: S3 versioning on with no lifecycle — delete is a lie.
