Consistency models

CORE

Definition

Strong, eventual, causal, read-your-writes, and monotonic reads — what users are allowed to observe as replicas and caches catch up.

Why it exists

Saying 'eventual consistency' is not a model; it is a shrug.

Approach

  1. Classify each read: must be linearizable, session-consistent, or eventually OK.
  2. After writes, return a version/token the next read must meet (RYW).
  3. Sticky routing or replay-from-leader for monotonic reads.

You say this first

Strong, eventual, causal, read-your-writes, and monotonic reads — what users are allowed to observe as replicas and caches catch up. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Linearizable: uniqueness, inventory decrement, leader election, permissions revoke.

Tradeoff: Stronger models: simpler product bugs, more coordination and latency.. Failure mode: RYW on server, broken by a second device or a CDN. Pick a model per API, not per company. 'Feed is eventual; follow graph write is quorum.'

More notes if you want them

Saying 'eventual consistency' is not a model; it is a shrug. Users notice going backward in time, not seeing their own edit, or friends seeing a comment in different orders. You need the named guarantees and how session stickiness or version tokens implement them.

Use when: Linearizable: uniqueness, inventory decrement, leader election, permissions revoke.

Skip when: Do not promise linearizability from a cache-aside read you just wrote through somewhere else.

Trap: RYW on server, broken by a second device or a CDN.
