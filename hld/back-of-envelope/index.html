Back-of-envelope: QPS, storage, bandwidth

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Back-of-envelope: QPS, storage, bandwidth.” They describe this:

Given: They said 100 million users and then waited. The next box you draw depends on the math.

Find: Turn DAU into peak QPS, storage/year, and NIC — loudly, to an order of magnitude.

Tiny example: 100M users, 20% DAU, 10 reads/day, 1 KB, 3× replication, peak 4×.

Name of the tool

Rough math that turns '100 million users' into QPS, disk, and NIC numbers so the rest of the design has constraints.

Why this tool

Interviewers are not grading your arithmetic to the last byte.

Approach

  1. Write the assumptions: users, DAU fraction, requests/user, peak factor, payload, retention, replicas.
  2. Compute write QPS, read QPS, then storage/day and storage/year.
  3. Compute egress and inter-service bandwidth; flag anything above a few Gbps per box.

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • How many QPS is that, roughly?
  • What is storage in year one?
  • Which number picks SQL vs object store vs CDN?

You say this first

Rough math that turns '100 million users' into QPS, disk, and NIC numbers so the rest of the design has constraints. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Every HLD interview, in the first 5–8 minutes after requirements.

Tradeoff: Conservative peaks over-provision and hide hot-key issues; optimistic peaks look elegant and page at launch.. Failure mode: Forgetting peak, retries, and bots — production QPS is not DAU/86400. Talk while you compute: 'I'll assume 100M users, 20% DAU, 10 reads/day… that's ~2.3k average read QPS, say 10k peak.'

More notes if you want them

Interviewers are not grading your arithmetic to the last byte. They want to see that you can convert a product story into capacity, pick a store that fits, and notice when a chatty design will melt a NIC. This is how you justify caches, shards, and CDNs instead of waving at boxes.

Use when: Every HLD interview, in the first 5–8 minutes after requirements.

Skip when: Do not spend 15 minutes on precise GB if the bottleneck is clearly consistency or a unique constraint.

Trap: Forgetting peak, retries, and bots — production QPS is not DAU/86400.
