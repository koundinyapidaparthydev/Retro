Monolith vs modular vs microservices vs SOA

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Monolith vs modular vs microservices vs SOA.” They describe this:

Given: The deploy is one repo and 40 people step on each other. Someone wants 30 services by Friday.

Find: Pick monolith, modular monolith, or services from team and failure boundaries — not fashion.

Tiny example: 12 engineers, checkout + recs + admin, recs can die without taking pay.

Name of the tool

Where you draw process boundaries — one deployable, modular monolith, service-oriented, or independently deployed microservices — and what that costs in ops and data.

Why this tool

Junior designs explode into twenty services on slide one.

Approach

  1. Start modular: packages/modules with their own tables and public functions.
  2. Extract a service when it needs different scale, language, or team cadence — and take its data with it.
  3. Define APIs and events first; no shared DB across extractees.

You say this first

Where you draw process boundaries — one deployable, modular monolith, service-oriented, or independently deployed microservices — and what that costs in ops and data. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Monolith/modular: small teams, unclear domains, early products.

Tradeoff: Monolith: simple txns and debug, coupled scale and release risk.. Failure mode: Chatty sync calls that recreate in-process function calls over HTTP. Say 'modular monolith first; extract X because of scale/team' — that is a senior line.

More notes if you want them

Junior designs explode into twenty services on slide one. Senior designs start with a modular monolith (or a few coarse services) and split when a team, scale axis, or failure domain demands it. Interviews want the criteria, not a fashion choice.

Use when: Monolith/modular: small teams, unclear domains, early products.

Skip when: Do not start a two-engineer product as twelve repos.

Trap: Chatty sync calls that recreate in-process function calls over HTTP.
