Multi-AZ vs multi-region

CORE

Definition

Availability zones are independent failure domains in one region. Regions are farther, legally distinct, and much harder for data. Do not confuse them.

Why it exists

Every serious design is multi-AZ.

Approach

  1. Default: 3 AZs for quorum stores and 2+ for apps.
  2. Pin a user's data to a home region when you can; serve reads locally from replicas/caches.
  3. Use sync replication inside the region; async or consensus-aware globally.

You say this first

Availability zones are independent failure domains in one region. Regions are farther, legally distinct, and much harder for data. Do not confuse them. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Multi-AZ: all production user systems.

Tradeoff: Multi-AZ: small latency tax, large availability win.. Failure mode: Single-AZ NAT, Redis, or Kafka. Draw two AZs minimum on every stateful box.

More notes if you want them

Every serious design is multi-AZ. Multi-region is a product and consistency decision, not a checkbox. Interviews punish 'we deploy to three regions' with no data story, and punish single-AZ Redis in an otherwise HA drawing.

Use when: Multi-AZ: all production user systems.

Skip when: Do not 'multi-region' a tightly consistent inventory without naming conflicts.

Trap: Single-AZ NAT, Redis, or Kafka.
