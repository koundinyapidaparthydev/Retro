The problem they give you

They will not say “Cache patterns: aside, through, behind, invalidation, TTL, stampede.” They describe this:

Given: You put Redis in front of SQL. After a deploy, the site is either stale or the DB is on fire.

Find: Pick aside vs through vs behind, and how a miss does not stampede.

Tiny example: 80k reads/s, 5% miss, TTL 30s, one key is a celebrity profile.

Name of the tool

Where the cache sits on the read/write path, how it goes stale, and how you stop a TTL expiry from becoming a database outage.

Why this tool

Caches are in every passing HLD.

Approach

  1. Default cache-aside + delete-on-write for OLTP objects.
  2. Version or namespace keys so deploys can ignore old shapes.
  3. Set TTL as a safety net even when you invalidate.

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • Cache-aside or write-through for this read?
  • Who invalidates, and when?
  • TTL expires on a hot key — thundering herd.

You say this first

Where the cache sits on the read/write path, how it goes stale, and how you stop a TTL expiry from becoming a database outage. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Read-heavy objects, sessions, rendered feeds, CDN assets.

Tradeoff: Aside: simple, stampede and stale-if-you-forget-delete.. Failure mode: Inconsistent key hashing so you cannot delete what you wrote. Name aside vs through and say 'delete the key on write, TTL 5m as backup.'
