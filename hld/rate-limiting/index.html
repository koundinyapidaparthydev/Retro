Rate limiting: token bucket, leaky bucket, sliding window

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Rate limiting: token bucket, leaky bucket, sliding window.” They describe this:

Given: One API key is 70% of QPS. Good customers are getting 429s too because the limit is global.

Find: Pick token bucket, leaky bucket, or sliding window — and the key you limit on.

Tiny example: Edge 100k QPS, per-key and per-IP, a NAT shared by a whole university.

Name of the tool

Algorithms that cap how often a client can act — protecting backends, enforcing fairness, and shaping 429s users can understand.

Why this tool

Without limits, one script or a retry storm is your capacity plan.

Approach

  1. Pick identity (key), algorithm, rate, burst, and whether to queue or reject.
  2. Implement with Redis or a limiter service; avoid per-pod-only counts for paid quotas.
  3. Apply coarse limits at CDN/gateway and fine limits in the service.

More notes if you want them

Without limits, one script or a retry storm is your capacity plan. Interviews love the algorithm names and then the distributed part: a local counter on each pod is not a global quota. This topic is the building block for the rate-limiter system design.

Use when: Public APIs, login, OTP, search, scrape-prone GETs, webhook inbound.

Skip when: Do not rate-limit yourself into dropping health checks or deploys.
