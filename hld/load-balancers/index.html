The problem they give you

They will not say “Load balancers: L4 vs L7.” They describe this:

Given: Twenty app boxes sit behind one VIP. Some connections are huge uploads; some are tiny GETs.

Find: Pick L4 vs L7, the algorithm, and what health means.

Tiny example: 50k conns, HTTP/2, one box has a stuck connection pool, WebSockets in the mix.

Name of the tool

A receptionist who hands each visitor to a free clerk so no one clerk is crushed.

Why this tool

You always draw a box in front of N app instances.

Approach

  1. Put a multi-AZ L7 LB in front of stateless HTTP APIs; L4 for raw TCP or TLS passthrough.
  2. Health-check a real dependency path; drain on deploy.
  3. Choose algorithm: round-robin, least-request, or consistent hash.

Whiteboard prompts

These are problem statements. The algorithm name is your answer, not the title of the question.

  • L4 or L7 in front of this app?
  • Round-robin vs least-conn vs consistent hash.
  • The LB marks a box healthy and it still 500s.

You say this first

L4 (TCP/UDP) sprays connections. L7 (HTTP/gRPC) understands methods, paths, headers, and can do routing, auth hooks, and graceful drains. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Any horizontally scaled app or gateway.

Tradeoff: L4: speed and simplicity, dumb routing.. Failure mode: HTTP/2 to one hot backend because L4 hashed the single connection. Say L7 in front of APIs and mention least-request + health checks.

More notes if you want them

You always draw a box in front of N app instances. The layer you pick changes TLS termination, sticky sessions, health checks, and whether you can route /video to a different fleet. Interviews use L4 vs L7 to test whether you know the packet vs the request.

Use when: Any horizontally scaled app or gateway.

Skip when: Do not use cookie stickiness as a substitute for a shared session store.

Trap: HTTP/2 to one hot backend because L4 hashed the single connection.
