Consistency vs durability

CORE

Definition

Consistency is whether replicas agree on a view of the data. Durability is whether a committed write survives crashes. They are not the same knob.

Why it exists

Candidates say 'strong consistency' when they mean 'do not lose the write.' A replica can be consistent with a leader that has not fsynced.

Approach

  1. Pick the ack condition: memory, local disk, majority disk, or cross-region replica.
  2. Pick the read condition: leader, quorum, any replica, or cache with TTL.
  3. Use write-ahead logs and replication so a follower can rebuild the leader's committed prefix.

You say this first

Consistency is whether replicas agree on a view of the data. Durability is whether a committed write survives crashes. They are not the same knob. I'd only add it if a requirement we wrote down actually needs it. Strong + durable: ledgers, inventory reservations, identity credential changes.

Tradeoff: Synchronous replication: better durability and often stronger reads; higher write latency and availability coupling.. Failure mode: Acknowledging the client before the write is on a quorum of disks. When they say consistency, ask: read visibility or crash survival? Then name both settings.

More notes if you want them

Candidates say 'strong consistency' when they mean 'do not lose the write.' A replica can be consistent with a leader that has not fsynced. A durable disk can still serve a stale secondary. Interviews reward people who separate 'what did I read?' from 'will this still be there after a crash?'

Use when: Strong + durable: ledgers, inventory reservations, identity credential changes.

Skip when: Do not use asynchronous replicas as the durability story for payments.

Trap: Acknowledging the client before the write is on a quorum of disks.
