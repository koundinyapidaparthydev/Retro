Idempotency and delivery semantics

CORE

The problem they give you

They will not say “Idempotency and delivery semantics.” They describe this:

Given: The client retried a 502. The queue redelivered. The user was charged twice.

Find: Pick at-most / at-least / exactly-once-enough and put an idempotency key on the effect.

Tiny example: Pay endpoint, 8s timeout, SQS at-least-once, a webhook partner retries for 24h.

Name of the tool

At-most-once, at-least-once, and exactly-once-enough: how retries and consumer offsets interact with keys so effects happen once.

Why this tool

Networks do not give you exactly-once.

Approach

  1. Client sends Idempotency-Key (UUID) on every mutating retry of the same intent.
  2. Server stores key → response/status with a unique constraint; replays the stored result.
  3. Consumers commit offsets only after the effect is durable, or use an inbox table in the same transaction.

More notes if you want them

Networks do not give you exactly-once. They give you retries and unknowns. Payments, inventory, and notifications are where juniors duplicate side effects. Seniors name the idempotency key, the dedupe store, and what still can happen twice.

Use when: Any payment, booking, notification, webhook, or 'create X' API.

Skip when: Do not promise broker exactly-once as a substitute for a unique payment_id.

Trap: LB automatic retries on POST without keys.
